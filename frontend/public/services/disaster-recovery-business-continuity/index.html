What is disaster recovery and business continuity planning?

Disaster recovery (DR) is the technical process of restoring IT systems and data after an outage, cyberattack, or disaster. Business continuity planning (BCP) is the broader strategy for keeping the entire organization operating - people, processes, and technology - during and after a disruption. Together, they ensure a ransomware attack, hardware failure, or natural disaster costs you hours, not weeks.

Most businesses discover their backup strategy has a gap during an actual incident - the worst possible time to find out. Veracity Technologies builds disaster recovery around verified, tested recovery, not just scheduled backups. That means immutable backup copies that ransomware can't encrypt, documented runbooks your team can follow under pressure, and recovery drills that prove your recovery time objective (RTO) actually holds up before you're depending on it.

FAQ

Common questions about Disaster Recovery & Business Continuity

How often should we test our backups?

We recommend quarterly recovery drills at minimum, and always after any significant infrastructure change - a backup that's never been tested for restoration is a guess, not a plan.

What's the difference between RTO and RPO?

Recovery Time Objective (RTO) is how long it takes to restore systems after an incident. Recovery Point Objective (RPO) is how much data you can afford to lose, measured by the time between your last backup and the incident.

Does cyber insurance require a documented disaster recovery plan?

Increasingly, yes. Many carriers now require evidence of tested backups and a documented recovery plan as a condition of coverage or to qualify for better rates.

What actually happens during a ransomware recovery?

We isolate affected systems, verify the integrity of immutable backup copies, and restore from the most recent clean point using the documented runbook - all coordinated so your team knows exactly what's happening at each step.
